Delivery Promise for the Website

Shoppers hesitate when they do not know when the parcel arrives. This module replaces that silence with one sentence on the product page, in the cart and at checkout: “Order within 3h 12m, delivered by Fri, 30 Oct”.

The date is not a fixed text. It is computed from the shop's own calendar: the daily cut-off, the days goods are prepared, the holidays the shop closes on, and the extra transit days of the destination country.

Where it shows

  • Product page — under the attributes, next to the price and the add-to-cart button, where the question is actually asked.
  • Cart — one promise for the whole basket, computed from the slowest line. A same-day item and a five-day item do not add up to five days: the basket leaves the warehouse when the five-day item is ready.
  • Checkout — the same sentence above the address block. The destination country is known there, so its extra days are included.
  • Order — when the order is confirmed, the promise is written on it as the commitment date, unless the merchant already set one.

How the date is computed

  1. The preparation time of the product, in working days. Products without their own value follow the Sales Lead Time, so the promise never drifts away from the lead time the rest of the company uses. Zero means “ships today”.
  2. The cut-off. Before it, preparation starts today; after it, on the next working day. One minute either side of the cut-off is the difference between this week and next week, so the comparison is inclusive and tested to the minute.
  3. Closed days. Saturdays and Sundays are closed by default and every day of the week is a checkbox, so a shop that closes on Friday evening is one tick away. Holidays are read from a list per website and skipped the same way, including several consecutive days and a holiday that falls on a Saturday anyway.
  4. The destination country, when it has extra transit days. They are added on top of the preparation time and walked over closed days too.

Timezone

The cut-off is read in the website's own timezone. This matters more than it looks: a shop in UTC+8 whose promise is computed in UTC sees 07:30 when the shop clock says 15:30, so every order placed after 16:00 local time would be promised a day too early. The timezone sits next to the cut-off in the website settings and defaults to the company's.

Settling the edges before your customers do

  • A Friday order placed after the cut-off with one preparation day lands on Tuesday, not on Saturday.
  • 31 December after the cut-off is promised in the next year, and a 1 January holiday moves it again.
  • A holiday that falls on a Saturday changes nothing, because the day was already closed.
  • An order placed on a day the shop does not prepare starts from the next open day, even if the cut-off has not passed.
  • A product whose preparation time is zero still ships today when the order is placed before the cut-off, and tomorrow when it is not.
  • Turning the promise off for a website removes it everywhere: product page, cart and checkout.

Setting it up

  • Website → Configuration → Settings → Delivery Promise: switch the promise on, set the cut-off and the timezone, tick the days goods are prepared, and fill in the holidays and the per-country extra days.
  • Website → Configuration → Delivery Holidays and Delivery Days per Country: the same lists, for a longer calendar than the settings form is comfortable with.
  • Product → Sales tab: “Ships in (days)” next to the Sales Lead Time, with −1 meaning “follow the lead time”.
